Hi, I have graduated college this year. I’ve basically spent the entire year working my ass off and looking back, there are things I wish I did better. I’m logging my learnings here. Keep in mind that these thoughts are unpolished and came off directly from my mind (wanted to keep it raw). Here we go:

- It’s been tough, it’s going to get even tougher. Don’t forget where we started. Don’t forget we spent a month reading one paper, which we can read in 1 hour.
- Don’t rush anything. Take it slow. There are a lot of tortoises that can win. Moving fast is a distraction.
- Give it time, it can be anything; the one friendship that’s stagnant or your career that hasn’t panned out the way you wanted it to. Let time do it’s thing, keep moving forward.
- Don’t let other people’s opinions or feedback ruin your work ethic or your day. It’s not in your control what other people think.
- It’s okay to feel like you’re behind others. It’s okay to take it slow. It’s okay to just exist, and to watch others do their thing. Life ain’t a sprint, it’s a marathon.
- I don’t want to be famous, I don’t want to be an expert. I just want to be a human who loves his craft, a craft he found accidentally and wouldn’t trade anything for it. Titles don’t mean anything, at the end of the day you’re a human — let that sink in.
- There’s no right timeline, there’s no wrong timeline. **There’s only your timeline.** You only have one life, are you seriously gonna plan it like a Sunday afternoon? LIVE IT!
- Learn to do many things at once, but not too many. Know your limits and systematically grow them. As ukai sensei put it: **don’t push beyond your limits, learn to grow them**
- Learn to be open-minded. It’s okay to use the tools around you to get things done, you don’t have to like it. You just have to do it. Use the tools if it makes your job easier. Remember, your job isn’t a reflection of how good you are. For all you know someone can be from FAIR and doesn’t know how to write bubble sort (I hope that’s not me).
- **Learn to say I don’t know more often, let other people teach you** — be it life lessons or academic lectures it has proven time and time again for you to sit and deliver when it matters, not sit and learn.
- **Learn to say No more often**. It’s a sign of a person who knows what they want and what they expect. If more money comes as a result of misery and experiences that may not help you then it’s not worth it.
- Go through tough situations, spend months trying to do something and fail. It’s the best way to grow as a person, and as a researcher. Failure is not a setback to be ashamed of, but a scar to be proud of.
- You don’t need 100s of jobs, you only need one. You don’t need thousands of amazing projects, you only need one. You don’t need to be right all the time, you only need to be right once to make it to the top. Be patient until that happens.
- No two people are the same, no two people can ever be the same. We are similar in attributes but not in life experiences. Think about it this way, if our family duplicated itself cell to cell and god decided to replace you with someone else, even then there’s a low probability that what they go through is 100% of what you went through. **Everyone’s different, don’t compare**. The only thing you should compare with is yourself yesterday.
- You shouldn’t try being better than other people, a life in such a pursuit is never yours; because you gave it away the moment you decided to do that. **Be better than yourself.**
- Be okay with the fact that you may not get the spotlight for the majority of your life. Hell, even if you do there’s a very high chance that it’ll only last a small fraction of the time you spent grinding. **Remember that it’s the process that counts, not the result.**
- Be okay letting loved ones go, be okay letting them live their lives in the way they desire. You cannot control anything other than your choices, your actions and your reactions.
- **Set your rules, live by them, update them.** Don’t give permission to control your rules to other people. Set boundaries, say no.

Overall, I think 2025 can be summed up in one sentence: **it’s the start, but also the end**
